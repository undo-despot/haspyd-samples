This model needs WebGL, which this browser has turned off. The flat version of the figure works without it.

The page. Time runs across the paper over 8 s, and pitch runs away from you over three octaves, 110 to 880 Hz on a log scale. Each line on it is an arc: one voice whose pitch follows the line.

The reading bar. The gantry sweeps the page as the present moment. Where it crosses an arc, a slider takes that arc's height, which sets how fast that voice's drum turns. Here the drums turn at 1/100 of their true rate.

The drums. Each drum is engraved with one hand-drawn period of its waveform. The stylus reads it, a fader shows the arc's envelope, and the bus sums the three voices into the amplifier, its vector display and the speaker.

The real machine. On the UPIC (CEMAMu, Paris, completed 1977) composers drew waveforms, envelopes and then pages with an electromagnetic pen on a large tablet. The first versions calculated a page before you could hear it. From 1987 it ran in real time, with up to 64 oscillators.
